## Q1. Linear Density of FCC Along [100]

**Question:** Find out the linear density of FCC crystal in the direction [100] in terms of atomic radius R.

**Options:**

**Options**

- (a) 1 / (2R)
- (b) 1 / (√2 R)
- (c) 1 / (2√2 R)
- (d) 1 / (3√2 R)

**Correct option:** (c)

**Formulas:**

N = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1
The segment length is a. For FCC, a = 2√2 R.
LD = N/a = 1/(2√2 R)

**Explanation:**

**Question:** Find out the linear density of FCC crystal in the direction [100] in terms of atomic radius R.

**Options:**

**Solution:**

For the [100] edge direction, the two corner atoms contribute:

N = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1

The segment length is a. For FCC, a = 2√2 R.

LD = N/a = 1/(2√2 R)

## Q2. Linear Density of FCC Along [111]

**Question:** Find the linear density for FCC crystal in the [111] direction in terms of the atomic radius R.

**Options:**

**Options**

- (a) 1 / (2√6 R)
- (b) 1 / (3√2 R)
- (c) 1 / (√2 R)
- (d) 1 / R

**Correct option:** (a)

**Formulas:**

N = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1
The body diagonal length is a√3. Since a = 2√2 R:
LD = 1/(a√3) = 1/(2√6 R)

**Explanation:**

**Question:** Find the linear density for FCC crystal in the [111] direction in terms of the atomic radius R.

**Options:**

**Solution:**

The [111] direction is the body diagonal. FCC has no atom at the body centre, so only the two endpoint corner atoms lie on this segment:

N = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1

The body diagonal length is a√3. Since a = 2√2 R:

LD = 1/(a√3) = 1/(2√6 R)

## Q4. Linear Density of BCC Along [110]

**Options:**

**Options**

- (a) √3 / (4√2 R)

N = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1

LD = 1/(a√2) = √3/(4√2 R)

**Explanation:**

**Options:**

**Solution:**

The [110] direction is a face diagonal. Only the two corner atoms lie on this segment:

N = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1

Length of face diagonal: a√2.

For BCC, a√3 = 4R, hence a = 4R/√3.

LD = 1/(a√2) = √3/(4√2 R)

## Q5. Linear Density of BCC Along [111]

**Question:** Calculate the linear density for BCC crystal in the [111] direction in terms of the atomic radius R. Consider the lattice parameter of the BCC crystal is a.

**Options:**

**Options**

- (a) 1 / (2R)
- (b) 1 / (3R)

LD = 2/(a√3) = 2/(4R) = 1/(2R)

**Explanation:**

**Question:** Calculate the linear density for BCC crystal in the [111] direction in terms of the atomic radius R. Consider the lattice parameter of the BCC crystal is a.

**Options:**

**Solution:**

The [111] direction is the body diagonal. It contains two corner endpoints and one body-centred atom:

N = 1/2 + 1 + 1/2 = 2

The body diagonal length is a√3. For BCC, a√3 = 4R.

LD = 2/(a√3) = 2/(4R) = 1/(2R)

## Q7. Planar Density of FCC (111)

**Question:** Calculate the planar density of FCC crystal for (111) planes in terms of the atomic radius R. Consider the lattice parameter of the cubic crystal is a.

**Options:**

**Options**

- (a) 1 / (2√3 R²)
- (b) 1 / (√2 R²)
- (c) 1 / (3 R²)
- (d) 1 / (4 R²)

**Correct option:** (a)

**Formulas:**

N = 3 × (1/6) + 3 × (1/2) = 2
A = (√3/4)(a√2)² = (√3/2)a²
PD = N/A = 2 / [(√3/2)a²] = 4/(√3 a²)
For FCC, a = 2√2 R, so a² = 8R²:
PD = 4/(8√3 R²) = 1/(2√3 R²)

**Explanation:**

**Question:** Calculate the planar density of FCC crystal for (111) planes in terms of the atomic radius R. Consider the lattice parameter of the cubic crystal is a.

**Options:**

**Solution:**

Planar density is the number of atom centres lying in the plane divided by the area of the planar repeat unit.

For the FCC (111) triangular section, the atom contributions are:

N = 3 × (1/6) + 3 × (1/2) = 2

The triangle side is a√2, so its area is:

A = (√3/4)(a√2)² = (√3/2)a²

Therefore:

PD = N/A = 2 / [(√3/2)a²] = 4/(√3 a²)

For FCC, a = 2√2 R, so a² = 8R²:

PD = 4/(8√3 R²) = 1/(2√3 R²)

## Q8. Planar Density of FCC Aluminium (100)

**Question:** Calculate the values of planar density of (100) plane for the FCC aluminium. Consider the atomic radius of aluminium is 0.143 nm.

**Options:**

**Options**

- (a) 1.22 × 10^19 m⁻²
- (b) 2.47 × 10^21 m⁻²
- (c) 4.43 × 10^25 m⁻²
- (d) 5.12 × 10^30 m⁻²

**Correct option:** (a)

**Formulas:**

N = 4 × (1/4) + 1 = 2
Area of the square face is a². For FCC, a = 2√2 R, so a² = 8R².
PD = 2/a² = 1/(4R²)
R = 0.143 × 10⁻⁹ m
PD = 1/[4 × (0.143 × 10⁻⁹)²] ≈ 1.22 × 10¹⁹ m⁻²

**Explanation:**

**Question:** Calculate the values of planar density of (100) plane for the FCC aluminium. Consider the atomic radius of aluminium is 0.143 nm.

**Options:**

**Solution:**

The FCC (100) face contains four corner atoms and one face-centred atom:

N = 4 × (1/4) + 1 = 2

Area of the square face is a². For FCC, a = 2√2 R, so a² = 8R².

PD = 2/a² = 1/(4R²)

R = 0.143 × 10⁻⁹ m

PD = 1/[4 × (0.143 × 10⁻⁹)²] ≈ 1.22 × 10¹⁹ m⁻²

## Q10. Highest Atomic Packing Factor

**Question:** Which of the following structure have the highest packing factor?

**Options:**

**Options**

- (a) FCC crystal
- (b) Simple cubic crystal
- (c) BCC crystal
- (d) Diamond structure (DC)

**Correct option:** (a), FCC crystal

**Formulas:**

- **FCC lattice parameter:** a = 2√2 R
- **BCC lattice parameter:** a = 4R/√3
- **Linear density:** LD = atoms whose centres lie on the direction / direction-segment length
- **Planar density:** PD = atoms whose centres lie in the plane / planar area
- **Atomic packing factor:** APF = volume occupied by atoms / unit-cell volume

**Explanation:**

**Question:** Which of the following structure have the highest packing factor?

**Options:**

**Solution and packing fractions:**

Atomic packing factor is the fraction of unit-cell volume occupied by atoms.

| Structure | Packing fraction (APF) | Approximate percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Simple Cubic (SC) | π/6 | 52.36% |
| Body-Centred Cubic (BCC) | √3π/8 | 68.02% |
| Face-Centred Cubic (FCC) | π/(3√2) | 74.05% |
| Diamond Cubic (DC) | √3π/16 | 34.01% |

FCC has the highest packing fraction among these options.
