## Q1. Nearest-neighbour distance in BCC

**Question type:** Crystal geometry.

**Question:** The nearest neighbor distance in BCC structure is:

**Options**

- (a) \frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2}
- (b) \frac{a\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}}
- (c) \frac{2a}{\sqrt{3}}
- (d) \frac{3a}{\sqrt{2}}

**Correct option:** (a)

**Formulas:**

- (a) \frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2}
- (b) \frac{a\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}}
- (c) \frac{2a}{\sqrt{3}}
- (d) \frac{3a}{\sqrt{2}}
**Formula(s):** BCC atoms touch along the body diagonal: \sqrt3a=4r. Nearest-neighbour distance =2r.
**Memory trick/Exam trap:** BCC nearest-neighbour distance is half the body diagonal; the radius is r=\sqrt3a/4.

**Explanation:**

**Question type:** Crystal geometry.

**Question:** The nearest neighbor distance in BCC structure is:

**Given:** Cubic lattice parameter a.

**Formula(s):** BCC atoms touch along the body diagonal: \sqrt3a=4r. Nearest-neighbour distance =2r.

**Intuition/Approach:** The body diagonal runs corner → body-centre → opposite corner. The corner-to-centre distance is half the body diagonal.

**Memory trick/Exam trap:** BCC nearest-neighbour distance is half the body diagonal; the radius is r=\sqrt3a/4.

## Q2. Unoccupied volume fraction in BCC

**Question type:** Packing fraction.

**Question:** The fraction of volume unoccupied in the unit cell of a BCC lattice is:

**Options**

- (a) \frac{8-\pi\sqrt3}{8}
- (b) \frac{\pi\sqrt3}{8}
- (c) \frac{6-\pi\sqrt2}{6}
- (d) \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt2}

**Correct option:** (a)

**Formulas:**

- (a) \frac{8-\pi\sqrt3}{8}
- (b) \frac{\pi\sqrt3}{8}
- (c) \frac{6-\pi\sqrt2}{6}
- (d) \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt2}
**Formula(s):** BCC packing fraction =\pi\sqrt3/8; unoccupied fraction =1-\text{packing fraction}.
1-\frac{\pi\sqrt3}{8}=\boxed{\frac{8-\pi\sqrt3}{8}}

**Explanation:**

**Question type:** Packing fraction.

**Question:** The fraction of volume unoccupied in the unit cell of a BCC lattice is:

**Given:** BCC unit cell.

**Formula(s):** BCC packing fraction =\pi\sqrt3/8; unoccupied fraction =1-\text{packing fraction}.

**Intuition/Approach:** Subtract the occupied fraction from the whole unit-cell volume fraction, 1.

\[
1-\frac{\pi\sqrt3}{8}=\boxed{\frac{8-\pi\sqrt3}{8}}
\]

**Memory trick/Exam trap:** BCC is about 68% occupied and 32% empty. Option (b) is the occupied fraction.

## Q5. FCC unit-cell volume from nearest-neighbour distance

**Question type:** FCC geometry.

**Question:** The nearest-neighbour distance in an FCC crystal is 0.2\,\text{nm}. Find the volume of the unit cube.

**Options**

- (a) 0.0226\,\text{nm}^3
- (b) 0.0289\,\text{nm}^3
- (c) 0.0312\,\text{nm}^3
- (d) 0.0412\,\text{nm}^3

**Correct option:** (a)

**Formulas:**

**Given:** d_{\rm nn}=0.2\,\text{nm}.
**Formula(s):** FCC atoms touch along the face diagonal: a\sqrt2=4r, and d_{\rm nn}=2r. Hence d_{\rm nn}=a/\sqrt2.
**Intuition/Approach:** Find a=\sqrt2d_{\rm nn}, then cube it to get the unit-cell volume.
a=\sqrt2(0.2)=0.2828\,\text{nm},\quad V=a^3\approx0.0226\,\text{nm}^3

**Explanation:**

**Question type:** FCC geometry.

**Question:** The nearest-neighbour distance in an FCC crystal is 0.2\,\text{nm}. Find the volume of the unit cube.

**Given:** d_{\rm nn}=0.2\,\text{nm}.

**Formula(s):** FCC atoms touch along the face diagonal: a\sqrt2=4r, and d_{\rm nn}=2r. Hence d_{\rm nn}=a/\sqrt2.

**Intuition/Approach:** Find a=\sqrt2d_{\rm nn}, then cube it to get the unit-cell volume.

**Calculation:**
\[

## Q6. Angle between [111] and [100]

**Question type:** Angle between crystallographic directions.

**Question:** Find the angle between [111] and [100] directions in a cubic lattice.

**Options**

- (a) 22.82^\circ
- (b) 43.47^\circ
- (c) 54.73^\circ
- (d) 82.12^\circ

**Correct option:** (c)

**Formulas:**

\cos\theta=\frac{u_1u_2+v_1v_2+w_1w_2}{\sqrt{u_1^2+v_1^2+w_1^2}\sqrt{u_2^2+v_2^2+w_2^2}}
\cos\theta=\frac{1}{\sqrt3},\quad \theta=\cos^{-1}(1/\sqrt3)=54.73^\circ

**Explanation:**

**Question type:** Angle between crystallographic directions.

**Question:** Find the angle between [111] and [100] directions in a cubic lattice.

**Given:** Directions [111] and [100].

**Formula(s):**
\[
\cos\theta=\frac{u_1u_2+v_1v_2+w_1w_2}{\sqrt{u_1^2+v_1^2+w_1^2}\sqrt{u_2^2+v_2^2+w_2^2}}
\]

**Intuition/Approach:** Treat the indices as vector components and apply the dot-product formula.

**Calculation:**
\[
\cos\theta=\frac{1}{\sqrt3},\quad \theta=\cos^{-1}(1/\sqrt3)=54.73^\circ
\]

**Memory trick/Exam trap:** For angles between directions, use the dot product. Do not subtract Bragg angles.

## Q7. Identify copper's crystal structure from density

**Question type:** Density and atoms per unit cell.

**Question:** Metallic copper forms cubic crystals with lattice parameter 0.36\,\text{nm}. If its specific gravity is 9.04 and atomic weight is 63.5, find its crystal structure.

**Options**

- (a) Simple cubic
- (b) Body-centred cubic
- (c) Face-centred cubic
- (d) Hexagonal closed packed

**Correct option:** (c)

**Formulas:**

**Given:** a=3.6\times10^{-10}\,\text{m}, specific gravity =9.04, M=0.0635\,\text{kg/mol}, N_A=6.022\times10^{23}\,\text{mol}^{-1}.
SG=\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\rm water}}\Rightarrow \rho=9.04\,\text{g/cm}^3=9040\,\text{kg/m}^3
\rho=\frac{ZM}{N_Aa^3}\Rightarrow Z=\frac{\rho N_Aa^3}{M}
Z=\frac{(9040)(6.022\times10^{23})(3.6\times10^{-10})^3}{0.0635}\approx4
**Memory trick/Exam trap:** Specific gravity is dimensionless. Convert it to density using water's density. SC/BCC/FCC have Z=1/2/4, respectively.

**Explanation:**

**Question type:** Density and atoms per unit cell.

**Question:** Metallic copper forms cubic crystals with lattice parameter 0.36\,\text{nm}. If its specific gravity is 9.04 and atomic weight is 63.5, find its crystal structure.

**Given:** a=3.6\times10^{-10}\,\text{m}, specific gravity =9.04, M=0.0635\,\text{kg/mol}, N_A=6.022\times10^{23}\,\text{mol}^{-1}.

**Formula(s):**
\[
SG=\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\rm water}}\Rightarrow \rho=9.04\,\text{g/cm}^3=9040\,\text{kg/m}^3
\]
\[
\rho=\frac{ZM}{N_Aa^3}\Rightarrow Z=\frac{\rho N_Aa^3}{M}
\]

**Intuition/Approach:** Calculate Z, the number of atoms per unit cell, then identify the structure.

**Calculation:**
\[
Z=\frac{(9040)(6.022\times10^{23})(3.6\times10^{-10})^3}{0.0635}\approx4
\]

**Memory trick/Exam trap:** Specific gravity is dimensionless. Convert it to density using water's density. SC/BCC/FCC have Z=1/2/4, respectively.

## Q8. Intercepts for an orthorhombic crystal plane

**Question type:** Miller indices → axis intercepts.

**Question:** An orthorhombic crystal has a=1.21\,\text{Å}, b=1.84\,\text{Å}, c=1.97\,\text{Å}. A plane with Miller indices (23\bar1) cuts an intercept of 1.21\,\text{Å} along the x-axis. Find the intercepts along y and z.

**Options**

- (a) 1.23\,\text{Å},\ -3.94\,\text{Å}
- (b) 1.43\,\text{Å},\ -4.26\,\text{Å}
- (c) 1.57\,\text{Å},\ -5.26\,\text{Å}
- (d) 1.76\,\text{Å},\ -6.12\,\text{Å}

**Correct option:** (a)

**Formulas:**

**Question:** An orthorhombic crystal has a=1.21\,\text{Å}, b=1.84\,\text{Å}, c=1.97\,\text{Å}. A plane with Miller indices (23\bar1) cuts an intercept of 1.21\,\text{Å} along the x-axis. Find the intercepts along y and z.
**Given:** a=1.21\,\text{Å}, b=1.84\,\text{Å}, c=1.97\,\text{Å}; indices (2,3,-1); x-intercept =1.21\,\text{Å}.
**Intuition/Approach:** The proportional x-intercept is a/2. To make it equal to the given a=1.21\,\text{Å}, multiply all three intercepts by 2.

**Explanation:**

**Question type:** Miller indices → axis intercepts.

**Question:** An orthorhombic crystal has a=1.21\,\text{Å}, b=1.84\,\text{Å}, c=1.97\,\text{Å}. A plane with Miller indices (23\bar1) cuts an intercept of 1.21\,\text{Å} along the x-axis. Find the intercepts along y and z.

## Q9. X-ray wavelength from Bragg reflection

**Question type:** Bragg's law.

**Question:** The spacing between successive (100) planes in NaCl is 2.82\,\text{Å}. X-rays give first-order Bragg reflection at glancing angle 8.8^\circ. Calculate the wavelength.

**Options**

- (a) 0.863\,\text{Å}
- (b) 1.12\,\text{Å}
- (c) 1.32\,\text{Å}
- (d) 1.54\,\text{Å}

**Correct option:** (a)

**Formulas:**

**Given:** d=2.82\,\text{Å}, \theta=8.8^\circ, n=1.
**Formula(s):** n\lambda=2d\sin\theta.
**Intuition/Approach:** First order means n=1. Substitute the spacing and glancing angle.
\lambda=2(2.82)\sin(8.8^\circ)\approx0.863\,\text{Å}

**Explanation:**

**Question type:** Bragg's law.

**Question:** The spacing between successive (100) planes in NaCl is 2.82\,\text{Å}. X-rays give first-order Bragg reflection at glancing angle 8.8^\circ. Calculate the wavelength.

**Given:** d=2.82\,\text{Å}, \theta=8.8^\circ, n=1.

**Formula(s):** n\lambda=2d\sin\theta.

**Intuition/Approach:** First order means n=1. Substitute the spacing and glancing angle.

**Calculation:**
\[
\lambda=2(2.82)\sin(8.8^\circ)\approx0.863\,\text{Å}
\]

**Memory trick/Exam trap:** Use the glancing angle \theta, not 2\theta, in Bragg's law.

## Q10. Smallest Bragg angle in NaCl

**Question type:** Interplanar spacing and allowed FCC reflections.

**Question:** For NaCl, the cell-edge a=0.563\,\text{nm}. The smallest angle at which Bragg reflection can occur corresponds to planes of indices:

**Options**

- (a) (100)
- (b) (110)
- (c) (111)
- (d) (200)

**Correct option:** (c)

**Formulas:**

**Question:** For NaCl, the cell-edge a=0.563\,\text{nm}. The smallest angle at which Bragg reflection can occur corresponds to planes of indices:
d_{hkl}=\frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}},\quad n\lambda=2d\sin\theta
d_{111}=a/\sqrt3,\quad d_{200}=a/2,\quad a/\sqrt3>a/2
| Q1 | (a) | a\sqrt3/2 |
| Q2 | (a) | (8-\pi\sqrt3)/8 |
| Q3 | (c) | (4w/\rho)^{1/3} |

**Explanation:**

**Question type:** Interplanar spacing and allowed FCC reflections.

**Question:** For NaCl, the cell-edge a=0.563\,\text{nm}. The smallest angle at which Bragg reflection can occur corresponds to planes of indices:

**Given:** NaCl has an FCC crystal structure with a two-ion basis.

**Formula(s):**
\[
d_{hkl}=\frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}},\quad n\lambda=2d\sin\theta
\]

**Intuition/Approach:** For fixed wavelength and order, larger d means smaller \theta. But FCC reflections are allowed only when h,k,l are all odd or all even. Thus (100) and (110) are forbidden. Compare (111) and (200).

**Calculation:**
\[
d_{111}=a/\sqrt3,\quad d_{200}=a/2,\quad a/\sqrt3>a/2
\]
So (111) gives the smaller angle among the allowed listed planes.

**Memory trick/Exam trap:** Check reflection conditions before choosing the plane with the largest spacing. For FCC, all indices must be all odd or all even.
